Before running the evaluation campaign for a Q1 robotics paper, which upstream design decisions of this simulation evaluation system should be changed, dropped, or kept, and in what order?

Synthesis

Where the council agrees

  • All five advisors: no campaign until image transport is fixed. Every SLAM-derived number so far (ATE, RPE, map completeness, loop closure) describes wheel odometry, so transport is the gate for everything else, and it must be proved with a received-vs-published check, not assumed.
  • All five: revert the 0.945 wheel_separation_multiplier and stop calibrating physics by fudge factor; treat odometry drift as something measured (Outsider: freeze physics and robot parameters before any metric work so results stay comparable).
  • Contrarian, First Principles, Outsider and Executor: the circular metrics must be dropped or redefined before running: responsiveness as defined, cleaning efficiency for an explorer, aligned map completeness as a headline, and ground-truth nav success at Nav2's own tolerance. Fewer, defensible metrics beat 14 compromised ones.
  • All five: run a 2-3 episode pilot and inspect it before the campaign; single-episode improvement claims (14% -> 62.5%, CI ~25-92%) are anecdotes and must not be reported.

Where the council clashes

  • Scope. The Expansionist wants to grow the paper (turn the failures into a benchmark contribution, open-source the harness, add a boustrophedon coverage planner now); the Contrarian, First Principles and Outsider want the smallest defensible claim with 6-8 metrics. A reasonable advisor could side with growth because a real coverage planner is the only way cleaning efficiency becomes meaningful, or with shrinking because nothing is trustworthy yet.
  • Fate of the circular metrics. Contrarian and First Principles say drop them; Executor and Outsider say redefine or relabel (responsiveness becomes controller latency; cleaning efficiency only once a coverage planner exists). Either is defensible: dropping is safest for review, relabeling keeps metrics the user explicitly asked for.
  • Campaign size. The Executor accepts 3 worlds x 10 seeds with Wilson or bootstrap intervals; First Principles says derive the episode count from a target confidence-interval width and probably cut to 2 worlds with held-out seeds. The first is simpler; the second is the only one that guarantees the intervals will be narrow enough to support claims.

Blind spots the council caught

  • Independent oracles for the metric code (four of five reviewers converged). The 87 tests re-encode the implementation; the council never proposed known-answer checks: synthetic trajectories with a hand-computed ATE, coverage and collision count, a replay of a real recorded bag, and ideally RTAB-Map on a public RGB-D dataset to validate the ATE pipeline.
  • Pre-registration and a development/evaluation split (four of five reviewers). Metric definitions, tolerances and exclusion rules must be frozen before campaign data exist, and Nav2/SLAM tuning must happen on development worlds and seeds, with the final campaign on held-out worlds and seeds. Otherwise every metric stays circular.
  • Per-episode validity telemetry and wall-time realism (three reviewers). Log image-drop counts, true RTF and a config hash per episode with explicit exclusion or rerun rules; check that seeds actually vary something meaningful; note that RTF and DDS drops interact, and that parallel subagents running on the same laptop compete for the CPU the simulator needs.

The recommendation

Follow the Executor's gated sequence, which all five reviewers rated strongest: fix and prove image transport, revert the physics fudge and freeze robot parameters, then freeze metric definitions, then pilot, then campaign. I am overruling the Expansionist's call to add a coverage planner and a benchmark-release framing now; it is sound for later but expands scope before a single trustworthy episode exists. On the drop-vs-relabel clash I side with relabeling: keep responsiveness only as wall-clock controller latency and cleaning efficiency only as a secondary, explicitly labelled number until a coverage planner exists, because the user asked for these metrics and honest relabeling preserves them without circularity. Add the reviewers' two blind spots before the campaign: known-answer oracle tests, and pre-registration with held-out worlds and seeds.

Do first: Raise the kernel socket buffers and Cyclone's minimum receive buffer, then run one 10-minute apartment episode and confirm that RTAB-Map's processed-frame count keeps rising with the camera frame count for the whole episode.

Round 1: Advisors

The Contrarian downside lens

The fatal flaw is the order of work. The campaign is the last step, not the next one. A 60-episode run (3 worlds x 10 seeds x noise on/off) costs about 45 min per episode, roughly 45 hours of wall time, and it would be built on a pipeline the critic has already shown is broken.

1. Finding (a) invalidates the whole paper. If RTAB-Map silently stops receiving images mid-run and there have been 0 loop closures ever, then "SLAM" results are odometry results. ATE, RPE, map completeness and loop-closure success would all describe the wrong system. Fix the transport before anything else: raise net.core.rmem_max, set a CycloneDDS or FastDDS config, or cut the subscriber count to the camera. Then prove that loop closures occur on a looped world. If they can't be made to occur, the SLAM metrics have to be dropped, not reported.

2. Don't "recalibrate" physics again. The 0.945 multiplier made yaw worse, and slip is non-stationary. Revert it and report odometry drift as a measured quantity.

3. Several metrics are circular or fake, and should be dropped rather than patched: responsiveness (e), cleaning efficiency (h), aligned map completeness (d), navigation success with the system's own 0.30 m tolerance (f). Fewer honest metrics beat 14 compromised ones.

4. 14% to 62.5% on single 8-goal episodes has a CI of about 25-92%; improvement claims need the full seed count or have to go.

5. "Fast progress with parallel AI subagents" is the trap. Subagent-written tests that re-encode the implementation are why 87 passing tests proved nothing. Before the campaign, run a 3-episode pilot and have a human check it against hand-computed ground truth.

The real question is whether this stack can support a Q1 claim at all, given that there is no cleaning planner and no baseline comparison.

The First Principles Thinker reframe lens

You are asking "which of the 14 metrics' upstream decisions do we fix, and in what order?" The right question is "what is the smallest claim this paper can make that survives review, and which pipeline layer must be trustworthy to support it?"

1. Right now there is no SLAM result. RTAB-Map stops receiving images, so there have been 0 loop closures and ATE/RPE just measure odometry. Any metric that depends on the pose or the map (ATE, RPE, map completeness, loop-closure success, coverage) is meaningless until image transport is fixed. It comes first, ahead of any metric design.

2. Physics is non-stationary: odom/GT yaw went 0.947 to 1.06 after a calibration. Revert the wheel_separation_multiplier and treat slip as the thing being measured. Do not tune it.

3. The campaign is the wrong unit of work. 60 episodes at roughly 45 min each is about 45 hours, and every finding (a-j) invalidates it. Instead run one 10-minute pilot per fix and check one thing: does the SLAM trajectory differ from odometry? Only then spend days.

4. Several metrics cannot be defended as they stand and should be dropped or demoted: responsiveness, cleaning efficiency, method comparison, and the 15 extras. A Q1 reviewer rewards 6 defensible metrics over 14 circular ones. Keep coverage, navigation success, collisions, ATE/RPE, loop closure and map completeness, but only after the denominator is reachable area and the evaluation is not scored on the map it was fitted to.

5. Statistics is a design input, not a post-processing step. With 8 goals the CI is about 25-92%. Define the episode count and goals per episode from a target confidence-interval width, then work back to how many episodes you can afford. Probably cut to 2 worlds and use a held-out seed set.

Order: transport, physics, metric validity (reachable denominator, frozen alignment, independent tolerance), pilot, campaign.

The Expansionist upside lens

1. The critic's findings (a)-(j) are the paper's most publishable result.
